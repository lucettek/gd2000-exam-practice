## 1. Is it a game?

The course defines a game as "a voluntary, structured play activity that
players do for fun." Pick one of Cookie Clicker, The Stanley Parable, or
Minecraft in Creative Mode. Argue whether it is a game under that definition,
and say which part of the definition it stresses or breaks.

Minecraft in Creative Mode
While creative mode is a lot less structured than survival, it still provides you the freedom to change, observe, and move throughout the same kinds of worlds presented in survival. Although there is not a clear objective, it gives you access to blocks, commands, and movement that is not available in survival and reinforces creative as a sandbox gamemode. The palette it gives you is structure enough to count as a game, as I don't believe that structure always has to come from a clear single objective.

## 3. Chess and Texas Hold'em

Both are two-player competitive games. Describe how hidden information and
randomness change what the player is actually doing in each. Then give one
concrete design consequence: something you would have to change if you moved
chess's rules to poker's information model, or the other way around.

For chess, there is no hidden information and the only randomness is what the opponent decides to do, which is not entirely random and can be predicted logically.

In Texas Hold'em, the two cards you have are hidden information while the five cards in the center are face up. Your cards and the cards placed down are random.

For chess, randomness and hidden information can be added by having the players have a randomized/assorted collection of pieces while keeping most of them hidden until they are played. For Texas Hold'em, the cards can all be face up but have new interactions between players to still make it engaging and exciting without the hidden information.

## 4. Tetris

In class almost everyone got bored before playing a full hour of Tetris. Why?
What would you change about the game to keep a player interested for that
long, and what would that change cost?

Tetris is an extremely simple game that is also repetitive. Although there is a high skill ceiling, it's hard for a lot of people to dive very deep into this game. A way to fix this would be to add new mechanics that include elements from other types of games like turn-based or platformers, and have core elements from those combine with the core elements from Tetris. However, that change would cost Tetris its simplicity, which is an iconic part of the game and a reason why it is so well known.

## 5. The game loop

Pick one of: Go, Blackjack, Settlers of Catan, Pandemic, Slay the Spire,
Hades, Minecraft, Stardew Valley, Overcooked, Pac-Man.

Describe its game loop: what the player does, what the game updates in
response, and what feedback the player gets. Then remove one step of the loop
and describe what breaks.

Game: Hades
The game loop in Hades is each run, where you spawn in the House of Hades and then go out to collect loot, boons, and fight enemies/bosses. Each time you die, you learn more about the enemies you encounter and things you collect and test during the runs. You can upgrade yourself permanently over the course of several runs which, along with the knowledge you gain about the enemies and strategies to use, allows you to progress further. If the permanent upgrades are removed, it fundamentally changes the genre of the game from a roguelike to a roguelite. Due to the fact that it is built for these upgrades, it would make the game much harder and more reliant on skill and luck than it already is.

## 6. Autobattler design

Dota Underlords and Hearthstone Battlegrounds are auto-battlers. Pick one
design rule -- for example, that a triple can only be ranked up once, or that
damage scales with the number of surviving units -- and explain the trade-off
it makes: what it encourages in the player, and what it costs.

A triple that can only be upgraded once disallows the player from focusing on collecting a singular character too much. A single upgrade encourages the player to build strength through their entire team/build, rather than relying on one strong character to carry the team. However, it could feel like too short of a progression path, especially in a longer game/round.
